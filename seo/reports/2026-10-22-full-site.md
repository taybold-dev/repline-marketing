## High Priority Issues

### 1. The app subdomain is indexed, and one of its pages ranks on page one

Search Console reports three `app.repline.io` URLs earning impressions:

| URL | Impressions | Position |
|---|---|---|
| `app.repline.io/auth/sign-up` | 56 | 24.25 |
| `app.repline.io/dashboard` | **26** | **9.38** |
| `app.repline.io/auth/forgot-password` | 4 | 15.00 |

86 impressions, **7.1% of all site impressions**, from pages that exist to be
logged into. A dashboard route is sitting at position 9.38.

This was invisible to five previous audits because they crawled
`www.repline.io/sitemap.xml`. The GSC property spans the whole domain, so it
sees the application too.

- **Impact:** three kinds of damage. Zero-value search results that waste the
  crawl budget and bounce every visitor who lands on them; a diluted brand SERP,
  where someone searching "repline" may be offered a login form or a dashboard
  instead of the marketing site; and, depending on what `/dashboard` renders to
  an unauthenticated crawler, a possible thin-content or soft-404 signal
  attached to the brand.
- **Fix:** `app.repline.io` needs its own `robots.txt` and `X-Robots-Tag:
  noindex` on authentication and application routes. This is a different
  codebase from this repo, so it cannot be done here. Then request removal of
  the three URLs in Search Console so they drop out before the next crawl rather
  than after it.
- **Effort:** Low in the app repo.
- **Note:** `www.repline.io/robots.txt` is correct and does not govern a
  different subdomain. This is not a regression in the marketing site.

### 2. The brand query converts at 2.6%, and brand is 38% of all demand

| Query | Impressions | Clicks | CTR | Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `repline` | **421** | 11 | **2.61%** | **5.52** |
| `rep line` | 19 | 0 | 0% | 2.47 |
| `repline meaning` | 5 | 0 | 0% | 35 |
| `reepline` | 4 | 0 | 0% | 9.5 |
| `repline store` / `replines` / `repil` | 3 | 0 | 0% | 39–83 |

Brand and its misspellings are 452 of 1,203 impressions and 11 of 29 clicks.

This is the one CTR number on the site that is unambiguous. A brand-name search
is the highest-intent query there is — the searcher has already decided who they
want — and brand terms normally convert many times better than generic ones.
**2.61% at position 5.52 on a 421-impression sample** is not noise, and it is the
largest sample anywhere on this property.

The research document predicted this from a collision with Replo (Shopify page
builder) and HockeyStack (B2B revenue AI), and ranked the fix eighth of
seventeen. It should have been first.

- **Impact:** roughly 400 people a quarter type the company's name and most are
  shown four other things before it. At a brand CTR even half of what is normal,
  the same impressions would produce several times the clicks — against a
  current total of 29 clicks per quarter sitewide.
- **Fix:** three parts, none of them content marketing.
  1. **G2 and Capterra listings.** Free, and no competitor in this category has
     them. They rank for brand queries, carry schema, and occupy SERP real
     estate that currently belongs to unrelated software.
  2. **Reorder the homepage title.** It is currently
     `Hockey Agent & Advisor CRM — Players & Contracts | Repline` (58 chars),
     which leads with the category and ends with the brand. For a query that
     *is* the brand, leading with it is likely to help. Something nearer
     `Repline — Hockey Agent & Advisor CRM` puts the matched term first. This is
     a hypothesis, not a certainty, and it is cheap to test.
  3. **`sameAs` on the `Organization` schema**, once social profiles exist, to
     give Google explicit entity signals that distinguish Repline from Replo.
- **Effort:** Low for the title, low-but-manual for the listings.

## Medium Priority Issues

### 3. Correction: `/features` is not the CTR defect this report called it

The 2026-10-12 report said `/features` — 76 impressions, position 8.05, zero
clicks — was "the clearest measured CTR defect on the site", and it was
recommended as the top action in chat. **That was an over-call, and the
arithmetic says so.**

At position 8, a reasonable CTR expectation is ~3%, giving 2.3 expected clicks
from 76 impressions. The probability of observing zero clicks purely by chance
is **9.9%**. A one-in-ten outcome is thin evidence of anything.

The same test applied to every page-one page earning no clicks:

| Page | Impressions | Position | Expected clicks | P(0 clicks by chance) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `/features` | 76 | 8.05 | 2.3 | **9.9%** |
| `/blog/chl-ncaa-eligibility-rules-2025` | 65 | 10.00 | 1.6 | 19.3% |
| `/blog/how-hockey-agents-and-advisors-network-with-scouts-coaches` | 26 | 7.31 | 1.0 | 34.6% |
| `/vs/hubspot` | 19 | 6.84 | 0.8 | 46.0% |
| `/vs/monday` | 9 | 6.11 | 0.5 | 63.0% |
| `/vs/spreadsheets` | 8 | 7.38 | 0.3 | 72.1% |

Not one of these is individually anomalous. Taken together — 203 impressions at
page-one positions, 0 clicks against roughly 6 expected — the combined
probability is around 5%, which is suggestive and nothing more.

For contrast, two pages are measurably **over**-performing, and those samples
are large enough to mean something:

| Page | Impressions | Clicks | Expected | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `/` | 613 | **19** | 9.2 | 2× expected |
| `/about` | 130 | 3 | 3.9 | on expectation |
| `/blog/nhlpa-agent-certification-guide` | 192 | 3 | 4.8 | slightly under |

The homepage title and description are working. The lesson for this report
series is the same one it has now learned three times: a number is not a finding
until it has been tested against what chance alone would produce.

- **Revised recommendation:** do not rewrite `/features` on this evidence. Note
  the pattern, re-check it at 90 days when the sample is three times larger, and
  spend the effort on the brand query, where the sample supports the conclusion.

### 5. Sitemap static entries still carry no date signal

Unchanged from 2026-10-12. `src/app/sitemap.ts` deliberately omits
`lastModified` on all 16 static entries, which fixed the always-today problem
and left no freshness signal at all. Blog entries correctly use
`new Date(post.updated ?? post.date)`.

Four comparison pages had pricing corrected on 2026-10-06 and three had copy
changed on 2026-10-15; the sitemap says nothing about either.

- **Fix:** derive per-route `lastModified` from git at build time.
- **Effort:** Medium.

## Detailed Findings

### Crawlability & Indexation — 77/100 (was 92)

- **robots.txt:** valid on `www`. Allows all agents, with explicit `Allow`
  groups for GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot. Sitemap declared.
- **Sitemap:** 27 URLs, valid XML, complete — 16 static routes plus all 11
  posts. No `/privacy` or `/terms`, both of which are `noindex`.
- **Canonicals:** self-referencing and correct on **27/27**.
- **Indexation:** `index, follow` on all 27 submitted URLs. The new `/privacy`
  rewrite correctly retains `noindex, follow`.
- **URL structure:** clean, lowercase, hyphenated, no parameters.
- **Crawl depth:** every page within two clicks of the homepage; no orphans.
- **Deductions:** −15 Finding 1 (app subdomain indexed), −8 Finding 5 (sitemap
  date signal).

### Technical Foundations — 94/100 (Core Web Vitals excluded)

- **Image sizing:** fixed and verified. Every fluid image emits the full
  eight-candidate srcSet; `/features` carries 39 candidates across five images;
  the hero preload carries matching `imageSizes`.
- **Image format:** WebP negotiated with `Vary: Accept` and a 4-hour cache.
- **Compression:** gzip on HTML, JS and CSS.
- **Payload:** HTML median 70.3 kB, max 119.0 kB. JS ~194 kB gzipped across 9
  chunks; CSS a single 9.3 kB gzipped stylesheet.
- **Rendering:** all 27 pages statically prerendered; full content without JS.
  `/llms.txt` is now a prerendered route (`○`). The only dynamic route is `/og`.
- **Security headers:** `X-Content-Type-Options`, `X-Frame-Options`,
  `Referrer-Policy`, `Permissions-Policy` set for all routes.
- **Mobile:** `width=device-width, initial-scale=1` on 27/27.
- **Mixed content:** zero `http://` references.
- **Unable to measure:** Core Web Vitals, production TTFB, HTTP/2 or HTTP/3
  negotiation, live redirect behaviour.
- **Deductions:** −6 for two low items (AVIF, unverifiable production TTFB).
